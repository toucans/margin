## Who buys it

| Buyer | Pain | Can pay | Verdict |
|---|---|---|---|
| Founders / operators under load | High | Yes | **Lead** |
| Coaches and therapists wanting method | Medium | Yes — they buy CPD | **Second** |
| "Spiritual seekers" | High | No | The résumé trap |
| People in acute crisis | Very high | Sometimes | Clinical, not mine |

Both live rows already pay an outsider for something adjacent — founders buy
executive coaching, therapists buy continuing education. Neither has to be
taught the category exists (P2).

**The failure mode is a slide, not a decision:** drifting from founders back to
seekers, because seekers are pleasanter to talk to and there are more of them.

## What decides it

1. **Purchasing power, set by which buyer — not which subject.** The only thing
   that kills this outright, and the one thing fully in my control.
2. **Subject barely matters.** Across 6,930 posts by people building this kind of
   business, market choice separates outcomes 45–67%; showing up repeatedly
   separates them 33–68%.
3. **English, not Thai.** P1 fails in Thai — no one is paying for it there.
4. **Price follows the named buyer, not the content.** *100M Offers*, the Dan
   Kennedy example: $19 generic, $499 named, on almost an identical product.

## The offer

**Narrow the promise, not the teaching.** One result, one buyer. What produces
the result stays interconnected in delivery.

**What is sold is the recurrence of the seeing.** A talk hands over an insight;
it fades in four days. Closing that gap needs another person, so it cannot be
had from a video — that is the whole reason it is paid.

Shape: small cohort or 1:1, 8–12 weeks, weekly contact, one behavioural
commitment per week, and a place to bring the week you got captured and be shown
the move you missed. Second shape, second buyer: method training for coaches and
therapists.

**Priced against the insight not evaporating**, anchored to executive coaching,
not meditation apps.

## The content

**A dhamma talk solves seeing.** One narrow problem of understanding, solved
completely — why the promotion didn't land, why the mind won't stop, the second
arrow.

The problem it reveals is always the same one: *I saw it clearly for four days,
then went back to being the same guy.* That gap is the offer. Content is the
proof, not the pitch.

## The funnel

Less funnel than expected, more list.

**Content → email.** The only step that matters early. A call CTA from cold
content converts badly and fills the calendar with the unqualified; the list is
still an asset in three years.

1. **Lead magnet** — the same logic in miniature. One narrow thing solved
   completely, runnable this week: a protocol for the first ninety minutes, a
   practice for the moment the urge to check hits. It works, it reveals the gap.
2. **Sequence** — three or four emails each solving something small, then one
   that names the gap outright (why insight doesn't stick, what does) with the
   offer attached. Then weekly, indefinitely.
3. **Application form**, not open booking. At this price the friction filters
   and inverts status before the call. Ask two real questions about their week —
   a straight *book a call* button converts worse, and the questions are
   themselves a dose of the product.

## The CTA

In nearly every piece, and soft. Talk, don't pitch, then one line naming who it
is for and what happens next.

Not *link in bio*. **"If you're the founder who's read all of this and still
can't put the phone down, I run a small group for exactly that — details in the
bio."** The specificity is the qualifier and the CTA at once.
