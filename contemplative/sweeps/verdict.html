Which framing has founder money

One of the eight has money aimed at founders on both platforms. It is burnout, named for the executive or founder. The framing you planned to lead with — the 2 a.m. decision — has no buyers anywhere I looked.

The answer, in one paragraph

Lead with burnout as the loop a founder can’t get out of. It is the only framing where someone is both addressing your buyer and paying to keep the ad running — Meta shows 158- and 279-day runs and a competitor with seven copy variants across five countries; LinkedIn shows founders being sold burnout work by name.

Keep “can’t switch off” as the second talk: proven, but only five advertisers on Meta actually speak to an operator. Treat drinking as evidence the mechanism sells — to someone else — and not as your offer.

The pattern worth acting on. On LinkedIn the ads that run 90+ days to founders sell keynote speakers, private staff and equity — not inner work. The ads selling founders burnout and psychology run 9 or 10 days. Founders appear to buy inner work in bursts, off a launch or a moment, not from an evergreen funnel. That argues for cohorts with real start dates over an always-on VSL.

Your buyer is reachable — just barely priced. Across 163,508 Meta ads only 8 advertisers both address a founder and run long enough to be paying for themselves. One operator, Clarity Group Global, runs long on both platforms. Uncrowded is the honest word; unproven would be too harsh, and busy would be wrong.

What this rests on, and where it’s thin

Meta is complete — 291 queries, 8 framings, 6 countries, 163,508 ads, zero errors. Every Meta claim above is measured. LinkedIn is roughly a tenth done: the full run aborted on an Apify billing cap after five keywords, all from C1, so the LinkedIn column rests on that plus two small probes of the burnout terms. C2, C3, C4, C5, C7 and C8 were never swept there. The C6 conclusion is therefore directional, not measured — it is the best-supported of the eight, on thinner evidence than I wanted.
