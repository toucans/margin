Where the money actually is

Two lanes are proven to pay. Neither of them is yours yet — and the lane closest to you is nearly empty, which is either your opening or your warning. I can't tell you which, and that's the honest headline.

Proven · but a different buyer

Drinking as the off-switch

The clearest money in the whole sweep. One advertiser ran a single creative 123 days across Britain and Australia at once, into a gated application form. A second operator runs the same hook from four different page identities into one destination across five countries — you only industrialise what pays.

“Have you noticed you’ve started relying on that evening glass of wine more and more to switch off at the end of the day…” Sarah Rusbatch · 123 days · GB + AU

The catch: the buyer is midlife women, not founders. What this proves is that the mechanism sells — people pay to stop a habit they use to switch off. It doesn't prove your buyer pays for it.

The 2 a.m. decision

The framing you planned to lead with. Across all six countries, essentially nobody is selling against it. What looks like volume is hypnotherapists and bathroom fitters whose copy happens to say “costs spiralling out of control”.

Empty means one of two things — untapped, or nobody can sell it. This sweep cannot separate those, and no amount of further sweeping will. Only a live test will: run the framing yourself and see if anyone raises a hand.

How sure I am, line by line
  • SolidThese ads ran for these many days. Measured from Meta’s own records, not estimated.
  • SolidThe sweep saw the whole market it could see: all six countries returned commercial ads, 291 queries, zero errors.
  • FairThat a long run means profitable. It’s an inference — a good one, and the premise you started from — but still an inference.
  • WeakPrices. The API gives only display domains, so I read homepages, not real landing pages. Ignore that column entirely.
  • UnknownWhether an empty cluster is an opening or a desert. Nothing in ad data can answer this.

The thing most likely to make this wrong

Founders and executives are usually bought on LinkedIn, not Meta. If that’s where your buyer is reached, then a thin Meta result says nothing about the market — only that I was looking in the wrong room. I have no way to see LinkedIn from here, and it is the single most plausible explanation for why the operator-facing lane looks so small. Treat “uncrowded” as a hypothesis, not a finding.

What I didn’t do: pull each surviving advertiser’s full ad set, so variant counts are my own copy groupings rather than Meta’s. Broad terms were capped at 500 ads per query, so the two largest clusters are sampled, not exhausted. One snapshot, one day.
