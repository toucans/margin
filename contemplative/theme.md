# The theme, and the tree under it

What the money in `ads.md` is actually being spent on, what that means for the
bounds in `contemplative.md`, and how far a hook can move before it changes the
room. Derived from the 44 advertisers who kept a creative running past ninety
days, plus the Hormozi corpus on how far hooks travel.

---

## The theme

**Something is running me, and I have already failed to stop it.**

Every surviving hook in the sweep names a process the person is *subject to*
rather than in charge of — thoughts that won't close, a body that won't settle,
a role that won't come off, a habit that runs itself. Not a goal they haven't
reached. A capacity they have lost.

That is the whole theme, and the two halves both matter:

- **Involuntary.** It continues without permission. The person is not choosing it.
- **Already tried.** Willpower has been spent and it didn't work. This is what
  makes the pain payable — if trying harder were available, they'd do that
  instead of buying.

Hormozi's first market test is *massive pain* — "they must not want, but
desperately need" — and his sales line is **"the pain is the pitch."** This
theme is that, in the one register that is constant, private, and daily.
([100M Offers](https://www.acquisition.com))

**What it is not, and this is the discipline:** not meaning, not purpose, not
insight, not optimisation, not growth. Nobody in 163,508 ads sustained spend on
those. The buyer is not reaching upward. They are trying to get something back.

## How much confidence this deserves

**Justified:** that these framings are worth money to somebody. Measured, not
inferred — 44 advertisers held a single creative 90 to 439 days across six
countries. Nobody funds a loser that long.

**Also justified:** that leaving the theme costs you. The framings outside it
were swept with the same method and returned almost nothing sustained.

**Not justified:** that *you* will make money by staying inside it. Demand
existing and you capturing it are different claims, and only the second one is
about you. The theme is a floor — it stops you spending years on a problem
nobody pays for. It is not a guarantee, and anyone selling you one is selling
the 25% (see *no formula*, below).

So: **stay inside this theme and you are at least talking about something people
demonstrably pay for.** That is the honest strength of the claim.

## The tree

```
                 SOMETHING IS RUNNING ME AND I CAN'T STOP IT
                                    │
      ┌──────────┬──────────┬───────┴────┬────────────┬─────────────┐
      │          │          │            │            │             │
  THE MIND    THE BODY   THE ROLE     THE HABIT   THE FRONT     (THE COLLAPSE)
 won't stop  won't settle won't come   runs itself  holds but      where it ends
                             off                     costs
    36%          34%         25%          11%          11%          outcome only
```

**1 · The mind won't stop** — 36% of survivors
- the decision that reopens at 2 a.m.
- replaying the conversation afterwards
- second-guessing something already decided
- lying awake with the day still running
- *proven:* Metacognitive Therapy, 120d, 3 countries —
  *"Overthinking is at the root of most of our mental distress."*

**2 · The body won't settle** — 34%
- wired and exhausted at once
- can't relax even when nothing is wrong
- the clench that arrives before the thought does
- *proven:* Dr. Sara B, 146d — *"Your nervous system isn't listening to you
  anymore."*
- **caution:** this is the mechanism branch. It is the most common connective
  tissue in other people's copy and the most tempting to lead with. `contemplative.md`
  says lead with the loop, never the mechanism — so this branch supplies
  *scenes*, not titles.

**3 · The role won't come off** — 25%
- holding it together for everyone else
- can't switch off at weekends, or on holiday
- still working in my head with the laptop shut
- *proven:* karl_williams, 160d — *"If you're a leader holding everything
  together for everyone else, but inside it feels like you're right on the edge
  of everything falling apart…"*; Mindstar, 151d — *"…even at weekends and even
  on holiday."*

**4 · The habit runs itself** — 11%
- the evening glass that became the off-switch
- the phone before the eyes are open
- checking the same thread again
- *proven:* Sarah Rusbatch, 123d, GB+AU — *"Have you noticed you've started
  relying on that evening glass of wine more and more to switch off…"*; and one
  operator running the same hook from four page identities across five countries.

**5 · The front holds, and it costs** — 11%
- looks fine, is not
- functioning, and hollow behind it
- *proven:* Kara Kuipers, 159d — *"You work hard to keep it together. And most
  days you do — but it costs something."* This is the theme almost verbatim,
  already surviving paid testing.

**The collapse — burnout — is not a branch.** It is where the tree ends. It is
the most crowded and least specific word in the evidence, and it names the
outcome rather than the experience. Use it as the consequence inside a talk,
never as the name of one.

**Outside the tree entirely** — swept, and found unpaid: meaning and purpose ·
enlightenment, non-self, surrender · productivity and optimisation ·
mechanism explainers · crisis and clinical.

## Can the hook move? Yes — and here is exactly how far

**Within a branch, freely and forever.** Hormozi is unambiguous that hooks
travel: *"I've used one of the same hooks in four different businesses that are
completely different. B2B, B2C, tech, and service. And it still works… you can
reuse hooks."*
([source](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=nljBRSUw-Eg&t=180s)) His working
ratio is **three to five angles, fifty hooks, 80% of them reworked from what
already won and 20% new**
([source](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=reisEL_D7xc&t=1992s)). That is the
supply model for hundreds of videos: few angles, endless hooks.

**Across branches, every swap changes the room.** This is the part the ad data
adds, and it is not in the Hormozi material because it is specific to here: the
five branches are currently **separate markets served by separate people**. Of
the ads selling drinking, 3 of 88 used any switch-off language. Of the ads
selling burnout, none named being unable to stop. Only 5 of 122 advertisers
appeared under more than one branch. Nobody is running this as one show.

So the practical rule:

> **The branch is the promise. The scenario is the illustration.**

You can absolutely have one video where someone reaches for a drink and another
where they reach for the phone — provided the thing being explained is the same
and the person addressed is the same. Alcohol is then an *instance* of reaching,
not the subject.

**The test for whether a scenario is safe to vary:** take the alcohol out and put
the phone in. Does the video still say the same thing? If yes, it was an
illustration — vary it as much as you like. If no, the drink had become the
subject, and you have quietly switched branches and audiences.

**One more, from the same source:** *"The easiest way to differentiate is to say
something no one else can say"* — Hormozi's argument for building content on
personal experience ([100M Leads](https://www.acquisition.com)). Yours is the
archive. Nobody else has it, and nobody in the sweep is using anything like it.

## How broad to be, and when to widen

Hormozi's shape is **puddle → pond → lake**: *"in the beginning you want to
artificially constrain the pond… you want to be the biggest guy in a puddle,"*
then widen as you outgrow it
([source](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=RY7U7XxPmMk&t=0s)). His only stated
limit on going too narrow: *"how do you know if you're too niche? If no one
exists that can buy your product."*

Against that, **"burnout and switching off" is not too broad — it is two
branches and an outcome, which reads as three shows.** Pick **one branch** as
the promise for the first long stretch, let the other four appear only as
scenes inside it, and widen when the puddle is visibly too small. The theme is
what stays fixed for hundreds of videos; the branch is what you commit to first;
the hook is what changes every time.

His other warning applies to the temptation that follows: *"don't make me niche
slap you"* — the failure mode is hopping branches because one felt slow, not
because you outgrew it.

**Organic does not buy you breadth.** The tempting premise — no ad money spent,
so a wrong viewer is free — is true about money and false about everything else:
organic reach is *earned*, so the wrong viewer costs the slot and the algorithm's
read of who you are. William Brown's micro-channel model is the sharpest version:
*"the right 500 viewers will always outpay the wrong 50,000 subscribers"*, and
*"if everyone thinks your content's for them, it's actually for no one."*
Hormozi lands in the same place — *"if you make content and you judge it by
views, I think that's dumb… the content is the targeting"*
([source](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=QR1q5645iTQ)).

**The one breadth both of them grant is awareness, not audience.** *"As you go up
funnel… you need wider hooks"*
([source](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=Mu7JlT34v1Y)) — a wider hook reaches
someone earlier inside the same problem. Reaching a *different* problem is the
other axis, and only that one changes the room.

## The branch committed to

**C2 — can't switch out of work mode.** It is the only branch where the proven
money and the intended buyer are the same person: karl_williams 160d, Kara
Kuipers 159d, Mindstar 151d, all addressing someone who cannot put the job down.
C5 and C4 carry more money and assemble a different room; C1 — the 2 a.m.
decision, the framing this work most wants to teach — has no visible buyer at
all.

C1 is not abandoned, it is **demoted from promise to scene.** C2 is what the
channel is about; the decision that reopens at 2 a.m. is one of the nights inside
it. The same demotion holds every other branch: the glass, the phone, the body
clenching first are Tuesday nights for the same person, and stop being separate
markets the moment none of them is the title.

**What crosses the line, concretely** — a habit named in the *title* (the drink,
the phone) switches you to that habit's market; leading with the mechanism
(*nervous system*, *regulation*) switches you to the most crowded lane in the
sweep; *burnout* as the promise rather than the consequence switches you to the
executive-and-HR market; meaning or purpose leaves the tree entirely. Note that
none of these is a demographic — the constraint is the problem addressed, never
who is allowed to show up for it (`contemplative.md` → *the avatar is a
recognition*).

## No formula, and stop looking for one

Tested directly against the sweep: **75% of the ninety-day survivors share no
opening structure**, and every structure that does appear is as common among ads
that died at 45 days. *"If you're X…"* is 6% of long runners and 3% of short
ones. Structure does not predict survival. **Specificity does.** Recorded here so
the question isn't asked twice.
